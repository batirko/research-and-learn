# Why it matters for your goal

An inspection is the only way to see what a colony is doing inside. Each one also costs the colony a morning of work, so you open a hive with a question and close it when you have the answer [source: association-handbook p. 6].

# The short version

- Open a hive with one question, such as "is the queen laying?"
- Look for eggs first, then larvae, then sealed brood, then stores.
- Work from the edge of the box to its centre, one frame at a time.
- Close the hive within about fifteen minutes on a cool day.
- Write down what you saw before you leave the apiary.

# Explanation

## Each inspection answers one question {critical}

A beginner opens a hive to see everything and closes it having seen little. The bees cover the comb, the light changes, and every frame looks alike. A question narrows the search. "Is the queen laying?" sends you to the frames in the middle. "Do they have enough food?" sends you to the frames at the edges.

**For your goal.** Write the question on your record sheet before you light the smoker.

## Read a frame from eggs to stores {critical}

Hold the frame over the box and tilt it to the light. Eggs sit upright at the bottom of a cell, like grains of rice. Larvae curl in a pool of food. Sealed brood sits under flat, brown wax caps. Honey sits under white caps near the top. [[B01]] explains why eggs come first: they tell you a queen laid within three days.

**For your goal.** One frame with eggs answers the commonest question. You can close the hive after it.

## What the record sheet is for {medium}

A record sheet turns single inspections into a trend. Write the date, the weather, the question, and what you saw on each frame you lifted. Compare it with the sheet from a week before. A colony that had eggs on four frames and now has eggs on one has changed, even if both visits looked busy.
