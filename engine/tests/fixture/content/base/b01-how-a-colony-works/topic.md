# Why it matters for your goal

Every decision you make at the hive is a bet on what the colony does next. You can't read its next move until you know how its members share the work [source: association-handbook p. 4]. The association's first-year course starts here for that reason [source: association-handbook p. 2].

# The short version

- A colony is one queen, thousands of workers and, in summer, a few hundred drones.
- The queen lays eggs. The workers do everything else, and change jobs as they age.
- A queen takes about 16 days from egg to adult, a worker about 21 and a drone about 24.
- A colony that loses its queen can raise a new one from a worker larva under three days old.
- So eggs and young larvae are the first thing you look for when you open a hive.

# Explanation

## One queen lays, and the workers do the rest {critical}

A honey bee colony behaves like one animal with thousands of bodies. The **queen** is the only female that lays eggs. The **workers** are females that don't lay. They clean, feed, build, guard and forage. The **drones** are males, and their one job is to mate with queens from other colonies.

The queen doesn't direct the work. Each worker answers what she meets: a hungry larva, an empty cell, a warm afternoon.

**For your goal.** A colony without a queen keeps flying for weeks. Judge it by its brood, not by the traffic at the entrance.

## Workers change jobs as they age {high}

A young worker cleans cells and feeds larvae. In her second and third weeks she builds comb, handles nectar and guards the entrance. In her last weeks she becomes a **forager**. The order bends when the colony needs it: a colony that loses its foragers sends younger bees out early.

**Bridge.** It works like a kitchen, where a new cook starts at the sink and moves to the stove.

## The brood clock sets the colony's deadlines {critical}

Every egg hatches after three days. What the young larva is fed decides the rest. A larva fed royal jelly throughout becomes a queen, and the others become workers. An unfertilised egg becomes a drone.

**For your goal.** When you find eggs, the colony had a laying queen within the last three days.

## Wild colonies nest in hollow trees {context}

Before people built hives, colonies nested in tree hollows and rock clefts. A hive copies the hollow: a dark, dry space with one small entrance. The frames are a human addition, so the beekeeper can lift the comb out and look.

# Positions

## Look for eggs before you look for the queen

**Why hold it.** Eggs prove a queen laid within three days, and eggs stay put while the queen walks away from the light. An unmarked queen can take a beginner the whole inspection to find.

**The strongest case against.** Eggs don't tell you whether the queen is healthy or old. Only seeing her, and her laying pattern, tells you that.

**What would change it.** A marked queen. Then a glance finds her, and the eggs confirm what you saw.
